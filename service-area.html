What the area has in common

The south suburbs and the South Side share a building stock: brick bungalows, two-flats, frame houses with brick porches, and mid-century subdivisions now on their second or third roof. They also share the same weather — the freeze-thaw cycle that opens mortar joints, cracks chimney crowns, rusts lintels and lifts driveways.

Forty-five years of working the same housing stock means we generally know what a street's houses are doing before we get out of the truck.
